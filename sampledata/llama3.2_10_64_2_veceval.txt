Influenza viruses contain segmented, negative-sense, single-stranded RNA genomes (Perofsky et al., 2024). This information suggests that the genome of Influenza A virus is composed of multiple segments of negative-sense RNA. The fact that it is a single-stranded RNA genome indicates that the genetic material is not double-stranded, and its negative-sense nature implies that the viral RNA is complementary to the host cell's mRNA (Wang et al., 2021).
