Nitrosomonas europaea, a bacterium involved in nitrification, utilizes the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate (HPC/HB) pathway as its primary metabolic pathway for obtaining energy. This unique pathway allows the bacteria to fix carbon and obtain energy from ammonia (NH4+), which is essential for their growth and survival (Chain et al., 2016). The HPC/HB pathway is a key adaptation that enables Nitrosomonas europaea to thrive in environments with limited organic carbon availability, such as wastewater treatment systems. This metabolic pathway has been found to be optimal at slightly basic conditions, with pH ranging from 6.0 to 9.0 (Chain et al., 2016). The use of this pathway by Nitrosomonas europaea highlights the importance of understanding the unique metabolic strategies employed by nitrifying bacteria in order to develop effective treatment strategies for wastewater and other environments.
