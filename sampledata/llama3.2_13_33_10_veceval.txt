Pseudomonas aeruginosa regulates the expression of its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin, through a complex system involving two-component systems (TCSs). These TCSs play a crucial role in controlling the production of various virulence factors, such as biofilm formation, motility, and secreted toxins (Sultan et al., 2021). The regulation of elastase and pyocyanin is primarily controlled by quorum sensing (QS) systems, which are complex signaling networks that allow bacteria to respond to changes in their environment (Kim et al., 2019). QS systems enable P. aeruginosa to modulate the expression of virulence factors in response to environmental cues, such as nutrient availability and population density.

The regulation of elastase and pyocyanin is also influenced by other TCSs, including those involved in biofilm formation and motility (Tortuel et al., 2022). These TCSs work together to fine-tune the expression of virulence factors, allowing P. aeruginosa to adapt to changing environments and evade host immune responses.

In addition to QS systems, other mechanisms such as phage variants and small RNAs also play a role in regulating the expression of virulence factors in P. aeruginosa (Chen et al., 2020; Pu et al., 2022). These mechanisms provide further insights into the complex regulatory networks that govern the expression of virulence factors in this opportunistic pathogen.
