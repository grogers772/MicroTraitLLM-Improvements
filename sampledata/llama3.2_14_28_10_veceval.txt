Nitrosomonas europaea uses the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate pathway as a unique metabolic pathway to obtain energy (Berg et al.). This pathway, also known as the "reversible citric acid cycle," allows Nitrosomonas europaea to convert ammonia into carbon dioxide and water, releasing energy in the process. This pathway is distinct from other nitrifying bacteria, which typically use the nitrite-nitrate pathway or the reductive ammonification pathway (Huang et al., 2018; Maza-Márquez et al., 2021). The reversible citric acid cycle allows Nitrosomonas europaea to thrive in environments with high ammonia concentrations, making it an important player in nitrogen cycling processes.
