Pseudomonas aeruginosa regulates the expression of its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin, through a complex system involving quorum sensing (QS) and two-component systems (TCSs). The QS system, which involves the production of autoinducers such as acyl-homoserine lactones, controls the expression of various virulence factors, including motility, biofilm formation, and cytotoxins (Sultan et al., 2021). Specifically, elastase and pyocyanin are regulated by QS systems, with elastase being controlled by the AlgR/AlgT TCS and pyocyanin being controlled by the PqsA/PqsR TCS (Chen et al., 2020).

In addition to QS, TCSs also play a crucial role in regulating the expression of virulence factors. The AlgR/AlgT TCS, for example, regulates the production of elastase and other enzymes involved in biofilm formation and motility (Kim et al., 2019). Similarly, the PqsA/PqsR TCS regulates the production of pyocyanin and other QS-controlled virulence factors (Pu et al., 2022).

The regulation of these virulence factors is also influenced by environmental cues, such as pH and temperature. For example, hypertonic glucose has been shown to inhibit the growth of Pseudomonas aeruginosa and attenuate its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin (Chen et al., 2020).

Overall, the regulation of virulence factors in Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a complex process involving multiple QS systems and TCSs. Understanding these regulatory mechanisms is crucial for developing effective therapeutic strategies against this pathogen.
