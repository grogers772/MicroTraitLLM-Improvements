Salmonella enterica uses type III secretion systems (T3SSs) to inject effector proteins into host cells during infection. Specifically, the T3SSs are molecular syringe-like machines that secrete virulence factors into host cells, allowing Salmonella to subvert host cell processes and maintain an appropriate intracellular niche (Lam et al., 2021; Pais et al., 2023). The two type III secretion systems encoded on Salmonella pathogenicity island 1 (SPI-1) and SPI-2 are essential for delivering virulence proteins across the vacuole membrane surrounding intracellular bacteria, enabling Salmonella to survive within phagocytic cells (Johnson et al., 2017; Yu et al., 2018). The T3SSs are central to Salmonella's virulence, allowing the bacterium to manipulate host cell functions and evade the immune system.
