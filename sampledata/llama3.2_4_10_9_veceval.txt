Based on the provided information, yeast is widely used in the field of brewing (Siddiqui et al., 2023) and is also grown in mild conditions to allow the growth of most microorganisms present in the air, water, or soil (Chen & Jiang). Additionally, yeast is commonly used in the production of bread fermentation (Reese et al., 2020). In beer production, Pichia kluyveri and Saccharomyces cerevisiae are often co-fermented to produce low-alcohol craft beer (Huang et al., 2024).

In bread production, yeast is a crucial microorganism for fermentation. It converts sugars into carbon dioxide gas, causing the dough to rise. In beer production, yeast ferments the sugars present in the wort, producing ethanol and carbon dioxide.

While specific bacterial strains are not mentioned as being commonly used in bread or beer fermentation, it's worth noting that other microorganisms like bacteria (e.g., E. coli, Bacillus spp.) and fungi (e.g., Debaryomyces, Kluyveromyces) may also be involved in the fermentation process.
