The CRISPR-Cas system plays a crucial role in Streptococcus pyogenes, a pathogenic bacterium that causes various infections. The system consists of a small RNA molecule called crRNA and an endonuclease enzyme called Cas, which together form the CRISPR-Cas complex (Rhun et al., 2019). This complex is responsible for defending against phage infections by cleaving foreign DNA, thereby preventing the phage from replicating within the bacterium. Despite its importance in phage defense, the biological role of the type II-A CRISPR-Cas9 system in Streptococcus pyogenes has received little attention (Beerens et al., 2021). However, recent studies have highlighted the significance of this system in modulating the expression of virulence genes and enhancing phage defense (Workman et al., 2024).

In addition to its role in phage defense, the CRISPR-Cas system has also been implicated in regulating the expression of other genes involved in bacterial survival and adaptation. For example, some studies have suggested that the CRISPR-Cas system may play a role in modulating the expression of virulence factors, such as toxins and adhesins (Lemaire et al., 2022). Overall, the CRISPR-Cas system is an essential component of the bacterial defense mechanism in Streptococcus pyogenes.
