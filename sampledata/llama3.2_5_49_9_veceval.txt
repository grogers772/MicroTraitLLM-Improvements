Vibrio cholerae produces a toxin known as cholera toxin, which has a primary effect of causing severe diarrhea in humans. The mechanism of action of cholera toxin involves the activation of adenylate cyclase, leading to an increase in cyclic AMP levels and resulting in the secretion of water and electrolytes into the intestinal lumen (Mekalanos et al., 2023; Pandey & Gerdes, 2005). This leads to severe dehydration and potentially life-threatening complications. The toxin is composed of two main components: the A subunit, which binds to GM1 ganglioside receptors on the surface of intestinal epithelial cells, and the B5 fragment, which activates adenylate cyclase (Pandey & Gerdes, 2005). Understanding the mechanism of action of cholera toxin is crucial for developing effective treatments and vaccines against this disease.
