Pseudomonas aeruginosa regulates the expression of its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin, through various mechanisms. The bacterium uses a complex system of regulatory networks to control the production of these virulence factors in response to environmental cues (Sultan et al., 2021). One key regulator is the two-component system, which plays a crucial role in controlling the expression of genes involved in quorum sensing and virulence (Sultan et al., 2021). Additionally, small RNAs such as AmiL have been shown to regulate quorum sensing-mediated virulence in Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1 (Pu et al., 2022). The bacterium also employs biofilm formation and motility as strategies to modulate the expression of its virulence factors. For example, hypertonic glucose has been shown to inhibit growth and attenuate virulence factors of multidrug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Chen et al., 2020).
