Pseudomonas aeruginosa regulates the expression of its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin, through a complex system involving quorum sensing (QS) and two-component systems (TCSs). The QS system, which involves the production of autoinducers such as acyl-homoserine lactones, controls the expression of various virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin (Schilder et al. 2016). Additionally, TCSs play a crucial role in regulating the expression of these virulence factors by responding to changes in environmental conditions (Tortuel et al. 2022).

The QS system is composed of two main components: the sensor kinase and the response regulator. The sensor kinase detects changes in the concentration of autoinducers, which triggers the activation of the response regulator (Sultan et al. 2021). This response regulator then activates or represses the expression of various genes, including those encoding elastase and pyocyanin.

Furthermore, research has shown that other regulatory mechanisms, such as the small RNA AmiL, also play a role in regulating QS-mediated virulence in Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Pu et al. 2022). These regulatory mechanisms highlight the complexity of P. aeruginosa's virulence factor regulation and underscore the importance of understanding these processes for developing effective therapeutic strategies.
