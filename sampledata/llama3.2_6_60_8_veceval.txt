Plasmodium falciparum is the parasite species responsible for causing malaria, a life-threatening and mosquito-borne infectious disease (Mwesigwa et al., 2024). Malaria is characterized by its severe form of infection that leads to high morbidity and mortality rates, with P. falciparum being the most significant cause of these outcomes (Jong et al., 2019; Jensen et al., 2019). The parasite's genetic diversity and multiplicity of infection are also key factors in its pathogenesis and severity (Mwesigwa et al., 2024).

According to a systematic review and meta-analysis, P. falciparum is the most significant cause of malaria-related deaths globally (Jong et al., 2019). The parasite's ability to infect red blood cells and evade the host's immune system contributes to its pathogenicity and virulence (Basco et al., 2023; Molina-Franky et al., 2022).

In summary, Plasmodium falciparum is a major cause of malaria, a disease characterized by severe morbidity and mortality rates.
