Escherichia coli (E. coli) is the most commonly used bacterial strain as a model organism in molecular biology research, including studies on genetic engineering and non-canonical amino acid synthesis (Jun et al., 2018). E. coli's simplicity and well-understood genetics make it an ideal system for developing and testing new technologies, such as genetic code engineering (GCE) approaches (Humolli et al., 2025). By focusing on E. coli as a model organism, researchers can develop and apply GCE tools to encode single and multiple non-canonical amino acids (ncAAs), which has significant implications for various fields of research (Cacace et al., 2023).

In addition, the study by Jann et al. (2024) highlights E. coli's potential as a model organism for studying genetic code evolution and modification, including the use of phages to introduce new ncAAs into the bacterial genome.

Overall, E. coli is widely used in molecular biology research due to its ease of manipulation, well-characterized genetics, and versatility in gene expression (Bechhofer & Deutscher, 2019).
