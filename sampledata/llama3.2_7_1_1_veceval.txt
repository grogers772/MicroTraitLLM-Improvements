Clostridium botulinum, a bacterium responsible for botulism, has a unique shape characterized by its rod-like morphology (Poehlein et al., 2015). The bacteria have a distinctive terminal spore-forming structure at one end and a rounded or oval-shaped body at the other end. This shape is often described as being cigar-shaped or egg-shaped.

Regarding oxygen requirement, Clostridium botulinum is an anaerobic bacterium, meaning it requires no oxygen to grow (Poehlein et al., 2015). In fact, C. botulinum thrives in environments with low oxygen levels, such as the gastrointestinal tract of animals or the soil.
