Salmonella enterica uses type III secretion systems (T3SSs) to inject effector proteins into host cells during infection. Specifically, the T3SS is a molecular syringe-like machine that allows Salmonella to deliver virulence factors into host cells (Pais et al., 2023). This system consists of two type III secretion systems associated with virulence, one involved in the invasion of host cells and another involved in maintaining an appropriate intracellular niche (Hotinger et al., 2021). The T3SS is essential for Salmonella's ability to survive within phagocytic cells and maintain its intracellular niche (Johnson et al., 2017). Effectors secreted by this system are crucial for the survival of Salmonella within host cells, as they allow the bacteria to evade the host immune response and replicate within the host (Naseer et al., 2022).
