Based on the provided information, yeast is widely used in the field of brewing (Siddiqui et al., 2023) and is also grown in mild conditions to allow the growth of most microorganisms present in the air, water, or soil (Chen & Jiang). Additionally, yeast is commonly used in bread fermentation as well. Specifically, Saccharomyces cerevisiae is mentioned as a fermenting microorganism used in various food processing fields, including beer production (Huang et al., 2024; Molinet et al., 2024).

In the context of bread fermentation, yeast such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae and other species like Debaryomyces, Kluyveromyces, Geotrichum, Mucor, Penicillium, and Rhizopus (Sharma et al.) are employed to produce lactic acid (dairy products, vegetables, cereals, meat) and ethanol/alcohol (baking, brewing, winemaking).

It is worth noting that while yeast is commonly used in both bread and beer fermentation, the specific strains and conditions used can vary depending on the application.
