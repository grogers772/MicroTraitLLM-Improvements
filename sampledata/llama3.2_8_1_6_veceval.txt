Escherichia coli (E. coli) is the most commonly used bacterial strain as a model organism in molecular biology research, including studies on genetic engineering and noncanonical amino acids (ncAAs). The use of E. coli as a model organism has been extensively documented in various scientific articles, highlighting its versatility and utility for studying various biological processes (Jun et al., 2018; Bechhofer & Deutscher, 2019). In the context of genetic engineering, E. coli is often used to develop novel enzymes that can produce ncAAs from supplied precursors, which has implications for biotechnology applications (Cacace et al., 2023).

The use of E. coli as a model organism is also supported by its ability to be engineered with various tools and technologies, including genetic coding expansion (GCE) approaches, which allow for the encoding of single and multiple ncAAs at the same time (Humolli et al., 2025). This has significant implications for understanding the mechanisms of ncAA biosynthesis and their potential applications in biotechnology.

Overall, E. coli is a widely used model organism in molecular biology research due to its ease of manipulation, genetic tractability, and versatility in studying various biological processes.
