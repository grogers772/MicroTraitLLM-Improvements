The CRISPR-Cas system plays a significant role in Streptococcus pyogenes, serving as a defense mechanism against phage infections (Beerens et al., 2021). Despite its importance, the biological significance of this system in S. pyogenes has received little attention until recent studies (Rhun et al., 2019). The CRISPR-Cas system is composed of two main components: the CRISPR array and the Cas enzymes. In S. pyogenes, the type II-A CRISPR-Cas9 system has been found to be particularly important in responding to phage infections (Shin et al., 2022). Research has shown that the CRISPR-Cas system can trigger a burst of CRISPR-Cas9 expression, which enhances phage defense (Workman et al., 2024). Additionally, studies have investigated the distribution and diversity of CRISPR-Cas systems in S. pyogenes, highlighting their importance in understanding the evolution and pathogenicity of this bacterium (Lemaire et al., 2022; Salvà-Serra et al., 2020).
