Nitrosomonas europaea, a bacterium involved in nitrification, uses the ammonia-oxidizing reaction as its primary source of energy (Huang et al., 2018). This process involves the oxidation of ammonia (NH3) to nitrite (NO2-) and then to nitrate (NO3-), releasing energy that is used for growth and division. The unique metabolic pathway employed by Nitrosomonas europaea is the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate pathway, which allows it to perform carbon fixation and obtain energy from ammonia (Berg et al.).

This pathway is distinct from other nitrifying bacteria, such as Nitrosococcus oceani and Nitrosospira multiformis, which have different optimal conditions for growth and metabolism (Tkaczuk et al., 2013). The ability of Nitrosomonas europaea to thrive in a wide range of environments, including slightly basic conditions with temperatures between 20 and 30°C, is also notable (Chain et al.,).

The use of this unique metabolic pathway by Nitrosomonas europaea highlights the diversity of nitrifying bacteria and their adaptations to different environmental conditions. Further research on these bacteria can provide insights into the mechanisms underlying their ability to obtain energy from ammonia.
