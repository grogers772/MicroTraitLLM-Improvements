Escherichia coli (E. coli) is the most commonly used bacterial strain as a model organism in molecular biology research, including studies on genetic engineering and non-canonical amino acid synthesis (Jun et al., 2018). E. coli's simplicity and well-understood genetics make it an ideal system for developing and testing new technologies, such as genetic code expansion (GCE) approaches (Humolli et al., 2025). The use of E. coli as a model organism has also enabled the discovery of novel enzymes that can produce non-canonical amino acids (ncAAs), which have potential applications in biotechnology and medicine (Cacace et al., 2023).

In addition, researchers have used E. coli to study the evolution of enzymes that require specific ncAAs for survival, such as through antibiotic pressure (Bechhofer & Deutscher, 2019). The ability to engineer E. coli strains with novel genetic modifications has also been demonstrated, including the restoration of phage-encoded genes in certain strains (Jun et al., 2018).

Overall, E. coli's versatility and well-characterized genetics make it a valuable model organism for molecular biology research.
