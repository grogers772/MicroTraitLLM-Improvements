Plasmodium falciparum is the parasite species responsible for causing malaria, a life-threatening disease (Mwesigwa et al., 2024). Malaria is characterized as the most severe form of the disease and is responsible for essentially all malaria-related deaths (Jong et al., 2019; Jensen et al., 2019). The infection with Plasmodium falciparum parasites causes the most severe form of malaria, which has a significant impact on public health (Ouologuem et al., 2023).

According to recent studies, Plasmodium falciparum is the primary cause of cerebral malaria, a condition that can lead to severe neurological symptoms and high mortality rates (Jensen et al., 2019; Basco et al., 2023). Understanding the pathogenesis and immune responses to Plasmodium falciparum parasites is crucial for developing effective vaccines and treatments against this disease.
