Mycobacterium tuberculosis is the bacterium responsible for causing tuberculosis (TB) in humans. This bacterium has infected humans for millennia and is a major public health concern worldwide (Parbhoo et al., 2022). The exact origin of M. tuberculosis is still debated, but recent studies suggest that it may have evolved from environmental bacteria that supplied genomic material to the human host, replacing historical arguments for a zoonotic origin (Koleske et al., 2023).

M. tuberculosis is an obligate human pathogen, meaning it requires specific conditions found in humans to survive and thrive (Sachan et al., 2023). The bacterium has developed various strategies to evade the host's immune system and persist in the body, including the formation of persister cells that can remain dormant for extended periods (Rahlwes et al., 2023).

Understanding the biology and pathogenesis of M. tuberculosis is crucial for developing effective treatments and prevention strategies against TB.
