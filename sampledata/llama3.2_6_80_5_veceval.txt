Plasmodium falciparum is the parasite species responsible for causing malaria, a life-threatening disease that affects millions of people worldwide (Mwesigwa et al., 2024). Malaria is characterized by its severe symptoms, including high fever, chills, and in severe cases, organ failure and death (Jong et al., 2019; Jensen et al., 2019). Plasmodium falciparum is the most virulent species of malaria-causing parasites, responsible for essentially all malaria-related deaths (Ouologuem et al., 2023). The parasite's ability to cause severe disease is attributed to its complex life cycle and the development of specific proteins that enable it to evade the host's immune system (Basco et al., 2023; Molina-Franky et al., 2022).
