According to the provided article, one of the key virulence factors of Helicobacter pylori that enables it to survive in the acidic environment of the stomach is urease (Baj et al., 2020). Urease is an enzyme produced by H. pylori that breaks down urea into carbon dioxide and ammonia, neutralizing the acidity of the stomach and creating a more favorable environment for the bacteria to thrive.

Additionally, other virulence factors such as sheathed flagella and adhesins also play a crucial role in the movement and attachment of H. pylori to gastric epithelial cells (Sedarat et al., 2024). These factors enable the bacteria to colonize the stomach lining and evade the host's immune system.

It is worth noting that the exact mechanisms of virulence and pathogenesis of H. pylori are complex and involve multiple factors, but urease is considered a key virulence factor in enabling the bacteria to survive in the acidic environment of the stomach (Ansari & Yamaoka, 2019).
