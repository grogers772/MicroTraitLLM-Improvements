Pseudomonas aeruginosa regulates the expression of its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin, through a complex system involving quorum sensing (QS) and two-component systems. QS systems, which involve the production of autoinducers such as acyl-homoserine lactones, play a crucial role in regulating the expression of virulence genes, including those encoding elastase and pyocyanin (Sultan et al., 2021). The QS system is controlled by a network of regulatory proteins that respond to changes in cell density and environmental cues. Additionally, two-component systems, which involve the phosphorylation and activation of response regulators, also contribute to the regulation of virulence factor expression (Sultan et al., 2021).

Furthermore, research has shown that QS systems can be influenced by phage variants, such as Pf4, which can reduce the expression of virulence-associated traits in Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Tortuel et al., 2022). The regulation of elastase and pyocyanin production is also influenced by environmental factors, such as pH and temperature, which can affect QS system activity.

In summary, the regulation of elastase and pyocyanin expression in Pseudomonas aeruginosa involves a complex interplay between QS systems and two-component systems, with environmental cues and phage variants influencing this process (Sultan et al., 2021; Tortuel et al., 2022).
