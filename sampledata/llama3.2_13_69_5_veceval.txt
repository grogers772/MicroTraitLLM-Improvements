Pseudomonas aeruginosa regulates the expression of its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin, through a complex system involving quorum sensing (QS) and two-component systems (TCSs). The QS system, which involves the production of autoinducers such as acyl-homoserine lactones, controls the expression of various genes involved in virulence, including those encoding elastase and pyocyanin (Sultan et al., 2021). Additionally, TCSs play a crucial role in regulating the expression of these virulence factors by responding to changes in environmental conditions such as pH, temperature, and nutrient availability.

The QS system is composed of two main components: the sensor kinase and the response regulator. The sensor kinase detects changes in the environment and triggers the production of autoinducers, which then bind to the response regulator, activating or repressing gene expression (Kim et al., 2019). In the case of Pseudomonas aeruginosa, the QS system is composed of several different TCSs that regulate the expression of various virulence factors.

Furthermore, research has shown that the QS system can be influenced by phage variants, such as Pf4, which can reduce the expression of virulence-associated traits in Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Tortuel et al., 2022). This suggests that the QS system is a key regulator of virulence factor expression in Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

In summary, the regulation of elastase and pyocyanin expression in Pseudomonas aeruginosa involves a complex interplay between quorum sensing and two-component systems. Understanding this regulatory network is crucial for developing effective strategies to control the spread of Pseudomonas aeruginosa and its associated virulence factors.
