Listeria monocytogenes, a pathogenic bacterium, utilizes various mechanisms to escape from the phagosome after being engulfed by a host cell. According to Osek et al. (2022), Listeria monocytogenes employs three main routes for transcytosis: invasion of goblet cells, para-cellular translocation involving Listeria adhesion protein (LAP), and through M-cells into the Peyer patches (Osek et al., 2022). Research has shown that Listeria monocytogenes can exploit the host cell's vacuolar system to escape from the phagosome, but the exact mechanisms involved are complex and multifaceted (Cain et al., 2023).
