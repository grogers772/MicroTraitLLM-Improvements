Based on the provided information, it appears that various microorganisms are involved in the production of bread and beer fermentation. However, no specific microbe is mentioned as being "commonly" used in both processes. The article mentions that bacterial mixed fermentation technology has been widely used in many food processing fields, such as bean products, fermented meat products, pickles, fish products, dairy, and alcoholic beverages (Siddiqui et al., 2023). This suggests that a variety of microorganisms are involved in these processes.

In the context of bread production, sourdough starters rely on a mixture of wild yeast and bacteria to ferment sugars and produce lactic acid, which gives bread its characteristic flavor and texture (Reese et al., 2020).

Regarding beer fermentation, various yeast species are used, including Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Pichia kluyveri, and others (Huang et al., 2024; Molinet et al., 2024). However, no single microbe is mentioned as being specifically "commonly" used in both bread and beer fermentation.
