Pseudomonas aeruginosa regulates the expression of its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin, through various mechanisms. Research has shown that quorum sensing, a process in which bacteria communicate to coordinate their behavior, plays a crucial role in regulating the expression of these virulence factors (Pu et al., 2022). Additionally, small RNA molecules, such as AmiL, have been identified as key regulators of quorum sensing-mediated virulence in P. aeruginosa (Pu et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2019). Furthermore, studies have also shown that biofilm formation and motility are influenced by the expression of these virulence factors, highlighting the complex interplay between these processes in regulating bacterial pathogenicity (Tortuel et al., 2022).
