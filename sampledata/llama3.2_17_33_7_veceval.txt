The CRISPR-Cas system plays a significant role in Streptococcus pyogenes, particularly in its pathogenicity and survival strategies. Research has shown that this system is involved in the defense against phage infection (Beerens et al., 2021) and can trigger a burst of CRISPR-Cas9 expression to enhance phage defense (Workman et al., 2024). Additionally, studies have highlighted the distribution, diversity, and roles of CRISPR-Cas systems in Streptococcus species, including S. pyogenes (Lemaire et al., 2022; Salvà-Serra et al., 2020). The review also emphasizes the link between CRISPR-Cas systems and bacterial virulence, as demonstrated by the study on S. pyogenes (Shin et al., 2022).

In terms of its biological importance in S. pyogenes, despite most applications emerging after the discovery of the type II-A CRISPR-Cas9 system, its role remains understudied (Rhun et al., 2019). However, research has shown that this system is present in various Streptococcus species and plays a crucial role in their survival strategies.

Overall, the CRISPR-Cas system in S. pyogenes is a complex and multifaceted system that plays a significant role in its pathogenicity, survival strategies, and interaction with phages.
