According to the provided information, one of the key virulence factors of Helicobacter pylori that enables it to survive in the acidic environment of the stomach is urease (Sedarat et al., 2024). Urease is an enzyme produced by H. pylori that breaks down urea into carbon dioxide and ammonia, neutralizing the acidity of the stomach and creating a more favorable environment for the bacteria to thrive.

Additionally, other virulence factors such as sheathed flagella and adhesins also play a role in the survival and attachment of H. pylori to gastric epithelial cells (Baj et al., 2020; Sedarat et al., 2024). These factors allow the bacteria to move towards the gastric epithelium and attach to it, facilitating the colonization and infection process.

It is worth noting that while urease is a key virulence factor for H. pylori, other factors such as lipopolysaccharide variation in the outer membrane also play a role in the bacterium's ability to evade the innate immune response (Sijmons et al., 2022).
