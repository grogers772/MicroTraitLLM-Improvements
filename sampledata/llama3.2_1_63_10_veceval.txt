According to the provided article, the Gram stain result for Staphylococcus aureus shows large, three-dimensional clusters that are difficult to bring into focus (Fisher & Mobashery, 2020). Under low magnification, the modified Gram stain gives insight into the colonization of infected tissues by detecting differences in either red/pink retained (Pseudomonas aeruginosa) or deep violet (Staphylococcus aureus) (Becerra et al., 2016). The cell morphology of Staphylococcus aureus is characterized as having a modified Gram stain pattern, with the ability to differentiate between Gram positive and Gram negative bacteria using the STAPH score, which combines Gram stain morphology and time to positivity (Hayashi et al., 2024).

The Gram stain readily differentiates the clusters as Gram positive or Gram negative bacteria, but there is a lack of contrast in the tissue in all samples (Gram stain image not provided). Routine Gram stain shows identification of Gram negative (Pseudomonas aeruginosa) and Gram positive (Staphylococcus aureus) bacteria in both large aggregates and individual bacteria within the biofilm (Fisher & Mobashery, 2020).

It is also mentioned that the STAPH score can be used to rapidly differentiate Staphylococcus aureus in blood cultures, with a prospective observational study showing promising results (Hayashi et al., 2024).
