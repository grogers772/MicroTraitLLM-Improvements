Listeria monocytogenes employs several mechanisms to escape from the phagosome, a critical step in its pathogenesis. Rapid phagosomal escape mediated by listeriolysin O (LLO), a pH- and cholesterol-dependent toxin with pore-forming activity, is a key mechanism for Listeria's intracellular replication and survival (Osek et al., 2022). Additionally, the production of endogenic factors, such as GILT (Gamma-interferon Inducible Lysosomal Thiol reductase), found inside the phagosome, facilitate vacuole degradation and bacterial escape to the cytosol (Sibanda et al., 2022).

Furthermore, research has shown that Listeria adhesion protein (LAP) plays a role in para-cellular translocation, allowing the bacteria to cross through M-cells into the Peyer patches (Osek et al., 2022). This mechanism is distinct from other bacterial pathogens, which are typically destroyed inside host cell vacuoles. The unique virulence mechanisms of Listeria monocytogenes enable it to evade the host's immune response and establish a successful infection.
