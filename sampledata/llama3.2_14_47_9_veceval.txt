Nitrosomonas europaea, a bacterium that plays a crucial role in nitrification processes, utilizes the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate (HPC/HB) pathway as its unique metabolic pathway for obtaining energy. This pathway allows the bacteria to perform carbon fixation and utilize ammonia as an energy source (Chain et al., 2016). The HPC/HB pathway is a novel mechanism of nitrogen metabolism that enables Nitrosomonas europaea to thrive in environments with limited organic carbon availability, such as wastewater treatment systems (Pedroza-Camacho et al., 2024).

This metabolic pathway involves the conversion of ammonia into nitrite and then into nitrate, which serves as an energy source for the bacteria. The HPC/HB pathway is characterized by a unique set of enzymes that facilitate this process, including the universal stress protein (USP) family (Tkaczuk et al., 2013). The presence of USPs in Nitrosomonas europaea has been shown to be essential for its ability to grow and thrive under various environmental conditions.

The HPC/HB pathway is a testament to the remarkable adaptability and metabolic versatility of Nitrosomonas europaea, allowing it to play a vital role in nitrification processes and wastewater treatment systems (Huang et al., 2018).
