Listeria monocytogenes employs several mechanisms to escape from the phagosome, a critical step in its pathogenesis. Rapid phagosomal escape mediated by listeriolysin O (LLO) is a key mechanism, allowing the bacteria to evade extracellular host defenses and survive within the host cell (Osek et al., 2022). LLO, a pH- and cholesterol-dependent toxin with pore-forming activity, facilitates the breakdown of phagosomal membranes, enabling the bacteria to escape into the cytosol (Cain et al., 2023). Additionally, GILT (Gamma-interferon Inducible Lysosomal Thiol reductase), found inside the phagosome, mediates vacuole degradation and bacterial escape (Osek et al., 2022). The combination of these mechanisms allows Listeria monocytogenes to evade host immune response and establish a successful infection.
