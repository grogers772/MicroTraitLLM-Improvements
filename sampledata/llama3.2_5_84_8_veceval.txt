Vibrio cholerae produces a toxin known as cholera toxin, which has a primary effect of causing severe diarrhea in humans. The mechanism of action of cholera toxin involves the activation of adenylate cyclase, leading to an increase in cyclic AMP (cAMP) levels within intestinal epithelial cells, resulting in excessive fluid secretion into the intestine and subsequent dehydration (Mekalanos et al., 2023; Pandey & Gerdes, 2005). This toxin is responsible for the severe diarrheal disease known as cholera, which can lead to significant morbidity and mortality worldwide. The production of cholera toxin by Vibrio cholerae is a key virulence factor that enables the bacterium to cause disease in humans (Vidakovic et al., 2023).
