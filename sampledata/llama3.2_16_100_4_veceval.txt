Salmonella enterica uses a type III secretion system (T3SS) to inject effector proteins into host cells during infection. This T3SS is composed of molecular syringe-like machines that deliver virulence factors into the host cell cytosol, allowing Salmonella to establish an intracellular niche and evade the host's immune response (Hotinger et al., 2021; Johnson et al., 2017). The T3SS is a key virulence factor for Salmonella, enabling it to manipulate the host environment and facilitate its survival within the host cell. Specifically, the Type III Secretion System Effector SptP of Salmonella enterica Serovar Typhi has been studied in detail, highlighting its role in modulating the host response (Johnson et al., 2017). The T3SS is a complex system that involves multiple components and effector proteins, but it plays a crucial role in Salmonella's ability to infect and survive within host cells.
