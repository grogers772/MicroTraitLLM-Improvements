According to the provided article, Influenza A viruses possess segmented, negative-sense, single-stranded RNA genomes (Perofsky et al., 2024; Alvarez et al., 2025). This type of genome enables reassortment, which is the interchange of genomic RNA segments when two viruses of the same type infect the same cell. The segmented nature of the influenza viral genome also allows for antigenic drift and subtype interference to shape epidemic dynamics (Perofsky et al., 2024).

It's worth noting that the article does not specifically mention the structure or organization of the Influenza A virus genome, but rather its characteristics as a segmented, negative-sense RNA genome.
