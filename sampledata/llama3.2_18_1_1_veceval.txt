According to the article, the mcr-1 gene is a plasmid-encoded resistance gene that confers resistance to carbapenems, a class of beta-lactam antibiotics (1). The mcr-1 gene is often associated with multidrug-resistant Escherichia coli (E. coli) strains, which can pose a significant threat to public health due to their ability to resist multiple antibiotics (2). The article states that the mcr-1 gene allows E. coli to survive and thrive in environments where carbapenems are used as a last resort for treating infections (3). This is particularly concerning given the increasing global concern of antimicrobial resistance, which can lead to treatment failures and increased mortality rates (4). In the context of E. coli, the mcr-1 gene has been identified in various clinical and environmental samples, highlighting its potential role as a key factor in the spread of multidrug-resistant bacteria (5).
