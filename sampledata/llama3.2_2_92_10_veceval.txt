Mycobacterium tuberculosis is the bacterium responsible for causing tuberculosis (TB) in humans. This bacterium has been a global health concern, affecting millions worldwide (Moopanar et al., 2022). The exact origin of M. tuberculosis is still debated, but research suggests that it descended from an ancestral mycobacterium that could successfully infect humans and remain dormant in their bodies (Koleske et al., 2023). Tuberculosis is a contagious infectious disease caused by the bacillus Mtb, primarily infecting the lungs, leading to the classic syndrome of pulmonary TB (Rahlwes et al., 2023). The bacterium's ability to establish infection and evade the host immune system is shaped by various factors, including host genetics, co-morbidities, environmental factors, and microbial virulence determinants (Joshi et al., 2021).
