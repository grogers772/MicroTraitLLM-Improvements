Salmonella enterica uses type III secretion systems (T3SSs) to inject effector proteins into host cells during infection. Specifically, the T3SSs are molecular syringe-like machines that deliver virulence factors into host cells, allowing Salmonella to subvert host cell processes and maintain an appropriate intracellular niche (Hotinger et al., 2021; Lam et al., 2021). The two type III secretion systems encoded on Salmonella pathogenicity island 1 (SPI-1) and SPI-2 secrete over 40 effectors that are essential for Salmonella survival within phagocytic cells (Naseer et al., 2022). These T3SSs play a central role in Salmonella virulence, allowing the bacteria to evade the host immune response and establish an intracellular niche (Aguilera-Herce et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2018).

The Type III Secretion System Effector SptP of Salmonella enterica Serovar Typhi is also mentioned in the article, highlighting the importance of this system in delivering virulence proteins across the vacuole membrane surrounding intracellular bacteria (Johnson et al., 2017). Overall, the T3SSs are a crucial component of Salmonella's virulence arsenal, allowing the bacteria to manipulate host cell processes and establish an intracellular niche.
