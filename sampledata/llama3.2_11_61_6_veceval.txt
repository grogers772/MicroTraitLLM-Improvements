According to the provided article, one of the key virulence factors of Helicobacter pylori that enables it to survive in the acidic environment of the stomach is urease (Sedarat et al., 2024). Urease breaks down urea into carbon dioxide and ammonia, neutralizing the acidity of the stomach and creating a more favorable environment for the bacteria. This virulence factor allows H. pylori to adapt to the harsh conditions of the human stomach and maintain a persistent infection.

Additionally, other virulence factors such as sheathed flagella and adhesins also play a role in the movement and attachment of H. pylori to gastric epithelial cells (Sedarat et al., 2024). These factors enable the bacteria to colonize the stomach lining and evade the host's immune system.

It is worth noting that while urease is a key virulence factor, other studies suggest that lipopolysaccharide variation in H. pylori may also play a role in innate immune evasion (Sijmons et al., 2022).
