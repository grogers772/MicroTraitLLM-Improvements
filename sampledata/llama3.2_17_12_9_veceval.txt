The CRISPR-Cas system plays a significant role in Streptococcus pyogenes, particularly in its pathogenicity and survival strategies (Beerens et al., 2021; Rhun et al., 2019). The system has been identified in various Streptococcus species, including S. pyogenes, and is thought to contribute to the bacterium's ability to evade phage infections (Shin et al., 2022). In fact, studies have shown that CRISPR-Cas systems can trigger a burst of CRISPR-Cas9 expression in response to phage infection, enhancing phage defense (Workman et al., 2024). Additionally, the system has been found to be involved in the regulation of virulence genes and the maintenance of genome stability (Lemaire et al., 2022; Salvà-Serra et al., 2020).

The CRISPR-Cas system is also thought to play a role in the distribution and diversity of Streptococcus species, with various subtypes I-C, II-A, and III-A being identified in different strains (Lemaire et al., 2022; Beerens et al., 2021). Furthermore, studies have shown that CRISPR-Cas systems can be used as a tool for the detection and typing of Streptococcus species (Shin et al., 2022).

Overall, the CRISPR-Cas system appears to play a crucial role in the biology and pathogenicity of Streptococcus pyogenes, and further research is needed to fully understand its mechanisms and functions.
