According to the provided article, one of the key virulence factors of Helicobacter pylori that enables it to survive the acidic environment of the stomach is urease (Sedarat et al., 2024). Urease allows H. pylori to break down urea into carbon dioxide and ammonia, neutralizing the acidity of the stomach and creating a more favorable environment for the bacteria to thrive.

Additionally, other virulence factors such as sheathed flagella and adhesins also play a role in the bacterium's ability to survive and attach to gastric epithelial cells (Baj et al., 2020; Sijmons et al., 2022). However, urease is specifically mentioned as a key factor in enabling H. pylori to survive the acidic environment of the stomach.

It's worth noting that while these virulence factors are important for H. pylori's survival and pathogenicity, they also contribute to the bacterium's ability to cause persistent infections (Ansari & Yamaoka, 2019).
