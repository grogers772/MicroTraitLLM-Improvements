According to the provided article, Influenza A viruses possess segmented, negative-sense single-stranded RNA genomes (Krammer et al., 2018). This type of genome allows for antigenic drift and subtype interference, which can shape epidemic dynamics in the United States (Perofsky et al., 2024).

In more detail, the article states that Influenza A viruses have a segmented genome consisting of eight to eleven genes, each encoding a different protein (Krammer et al., 2018). This segmentation allows for the virus to undergo antigenic drift and subtype interference, which can lead to changes in the virus's surface proteins and affect its ability to infect humans.

It is worth noting that the article also mentions that Influenza A viruses have a unique genome structure compared to other influenza viruses, such as H1N1 and H3N2 (Perofsky et al., 2024). The segmentation of the genome allows for greater genetic diversity among different strains of Influenza A virus, which can contribute to its ability to evade the immune system.

Overall, the article suggests that the segmented, negative-sense single-stranded RNA genome is a key feature of Influenza A viruses and plays a significant role in shaping epidemic dynamics.
