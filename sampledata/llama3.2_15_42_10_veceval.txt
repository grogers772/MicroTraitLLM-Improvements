Listeria monocytogenes escapes from the phagosome through rapid phagosomal escape mediated by listeriolysin O (LLO), a pH- and cholesterol-dependent toxin with pore-forming activity. This process is crucial for Listeria's intracellular replication and pathogenesis (Osek et al., 2022). The production of endogenic factors, such as LLO and Gamma-interferon Inducible Lysosomal Thiol reductase (GILT), allows the bacteria to evade vacuole degradation and escape into the cytosol. This cell invasion permits Listeria to evade extracellular host defenses while vacuolar escape is essential for intracellular survival (Sibanda et al., 2022). The rapid replication of Listeria in the cytosol, followed by the secretion of proteins, enables the bacteria to disseminate through the blood and target organs (Osek et al., 2022).
