Listeria monocytogenes uses several mechanisms to escape from the phagosome, but one of the most critical mechanisms involves rapid phagosomal escape mediated by listeriolysin O (LLO). LLO is a pore-forming toxin that creates pores in the phagosomal membrane, allowing the bacteria to escape and replicate within the host cell (Osek et al., 2022). This process is essential for the pathogenesis of Listeria monocytogenes, as it enables the bacteria to evade the host's immune response and establish a persistent infection.

In addition to LLO-mediated phagosomal escape, other mechanisms such as transcytosis through goblet cells or para-cellular translocation involving Listeria adhesion protein (LAP) can also contribute to the pathogenesis of Listeria monocytogenes (Osek et al., 2022). However, rapid phagosomal escape mediated by LLO is a key mechanism that allows the bacteria to establish an intracellular replication cycle and evade the host's immune response.
