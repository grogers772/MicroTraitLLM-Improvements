The CRISPR-Cas system plays a significant role in Streptococcus pyogenes, serving as a defense mechanism against phage infections (Rhun et al., 2019). Despite its importance, the biological significance of the type II-A CRISPR-Cas9 system in S. pyogenes has received little attention (Beerens et al., 2021). However, research has shown that this system is involved in the regulation of virulence factors and the modulation of the host immune response (Lemaire et al., 2022). The distribution and diversity of CRISPR-Cas systems among S. pyogenes species have also been studied, highlighting their potential applications in understanding the pathogenicity of these bacteria (Shin et al., 2022).

In addition to its role in phage defense, the CRISPR-Cas system has been implicated in the regulation of gene expression and the maintenance of genome stability in S. pyogenes (Workman et al., 2024). The study of CRISPR-Cas systems in S. pyogenes provides valuable insights into the evolution of this bacterium's virulence factors and its ability to adapt to changing environments.
