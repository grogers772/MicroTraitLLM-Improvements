Pseudomonas aeruginosa regulates the expression of its virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin, through a complex system involving quorum sensing (QS) and two-component systems (TCSs). The QS system, which involves the production of autoinducers such as acyl-homoserine lactones, controls the expression of various virulence factors, including elastase and pyocyanin (Sultan et al., 2021). Additionally, TCSs play a crucial role in regulating the expression of these virulence factors by responding to changes in environmental conditions. For example, the Pqs system, a QS system in Pseudomonas aeruginosa, regulates the production of elastase and pyocyanin (Kim et al., 2019). Furthermore, studies have shown that other TCSs, such as the AlgR/AlgU pathway, also regulate the expression of virulence factors in Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Sultan et al., 2021).

In addition to QS and TCSs, other regulatory mechanisms, such as the presence of phage variants, have been shown to influence the regulation of virulence factor expression in Pseudomonas aeruginosa. For example, a study found that the Pf4 phage variant infection reduced the expression of virulence-associated traits in Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Tortuel et al., 2022). Overall, the regulation of virulence factor expression in Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a complex process involving multiple regulatory mechanisms.
