Vibrio cholerae produces a major toxin known as cholera toxin, which has a significant impact on the human body. The primary effect of cholera toxin is to cause severe diarrhea by activating adenylate cyclase in intestinal epithelial cells, leading to an increase in cyclic AMP levels and subsequent secretion of chloride ions into the lumen (Pandey & Gerdes, 2005). This results in a massive loss of fluids and electrolytes, which can lead to dehydration and potentially life-threatening complications. The toxin's primary effect is to disrupt the normal functioning of intestinal epithelial cells, causing an excessive release of fluid and electrolytes into the intestine.

Additionally, research has shown that Vibrio cholerae also produces other toxins, such as cholera toxin-adjacent protein (CTA) and enterotoxin-like toxin (ETLT), which can contribute to the pathogenesis of the disease (Montero et al., 2023). However, cholera toxin is the most well-studied and widely recognized toxin produced by Vibrio cholerae.
