Salmonella enterica uses type III secretion systems (T3SSs) to inject effector proteins into host cells during infection. Specifically, the T3SS is a molecular syringe-like machine that allows Salmonella to deliver virulence factors into host cells (Hotinger et al., 2021). This system consists of specialized nanomachines known as type III secretion systems (T3SSs) that inject effectors into the host cell cytosol, allowing Salmonella to maintain an appropriate intracellular niche and evade the host's immune response (Pais et al., 2023).

The T3SS is composed of two main components: the needle-like structure that penetrates the host cell membrane and the effector proteins that are injected into the host cell cytosol. The Type III Secretion System Effector SptP of Salmonella enterica Serovar Typhi has been studied extensively, and its role in maintaining SCV (Salmonella-containing vacuole) and allowing Salmonella survival within phagocytic cells is well established (Johnson et al., 2017).

The use of T3SSs by Salmonella allows the bacterium to manipulate the host cell environment and evade the immune response, ultimately leading to infection and disease. Understanding the mechanisms of T3SSs in Salmonella is crucial for developing effective therapeutic strategies against this pathogen.
